Today, I am writing regex code to detect email addresses.

I am using the regex pattern `\b[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+@[A-Za-z0-9.-]+\.[A-Za-z]{2,}\b`. This pattern is based on the standard email format: `username@domain.extension`. Here is the explanation: `\b` serves as a boundary marker to ensure a match does not occur in the middle of a word; `[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+` allows for a combination of uppercase letters (A-Z), lowercase letters (a-z), numbers (0-9), and special characters—specifically the underscore (\_), percent (%), plus (+), and hyphen (-)—for the _username_ part; `+@` ensures there is at least one character before the `@` symbol; `[A-Za-z0-9.-]+` allows for a combination of letters and numbers for the _domain_ part; and `\.[A-Za-z]{2,}` ensures the presence of a dot followed by at least two letters for the extension part. The backslash character `\` is used before the dot (`\.`) because a standard dot in regex has a special meaning (representing any character), so `\.` is required to match the actual dot character. Finally, `{2,}` specifies that the extension part must consist of at least two alphabetic characters, and my proof is located in the images section under the "Week 2" subsection, with the filename 'Email Detection using Regex.png'.
